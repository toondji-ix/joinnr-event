## Research protocol

Proposed starting sample, subject to owner approval: six to eight attendees across first-time/returning and seek/offer goals, two organizers from separate prospective pilots, and two planners or operational delegates. Include participants with accessibility needs, low-bandwidth constraints and varied iOS/Android/web preferences where recruitment allows. These are planning counts, not statistical power or validation thresholds. Report actual coverage and gaps; convenience samples and organizer-selected attendees can bias results. If no further interviews change the findings, record why the team considers evidence sufficient and what remains uncertain.

Before each session explain purpose, voluntary participation, ability to skip/stop, intended use, who can access notes, and the approved retention/deletion process. Ask separately for recording permission; default to notes without recordings. If research handling rules are not approved, prepare instruments but defer collection. Do not gather birth dates, real contact lists, sensitive traits or assessment answers. Use participant codes in synthesis; keep recruitment identities and contact details in approved restricted storage outside Git. Do not commit raw notes, recordings or identifiable quotations. Share only reviewed deidentified summaries and restricted evidence references.

### Attendee interview guide

Allow approximately 30 minutes, adjusted to participant needs. Use neutral prompts and follow concrete examples; do not ask participants to endorse a promised benefit.

1. Describe the last eligible conference you attended and what you hoped to gain. How did you find people to speak with?
2. Walk through your most recent useful introduction and an unsuccessful attempt. What happened, how much effort did it take, and what made the outcome useful?
3. What tools or workarounds did you use? When did you stop trying? What would make you keep the current approach?
4. What information would help you choose a conversation? What would you avoid sharing with attendees, organizers or employers?
5. Using a verbal concept or clearly labelled paper sketch, ask what they understand about an event-only shortlist and its reasons. What would make a recommendation unhelpful or unsafe?
6. Ask them to explain joining, a connection request and mutual contact permission in their own words. What do they expect after decline, block, revoke, departure or event close?
7. Ask which profile steps they would actually complete, which they would skip and when. Probe optional company/photo/experience/style, private goals, and what skipping should do. Do not imply validated personality science.
8. How would they access this at the venue? Probe installation resistance, camera denial/manual code, assistive technology, device, connectivity and time constraints.
9. What useful outcome could they report afterward? What would prevent responding? End with concerns and reasons they might decline participation.

Record stated willingness separately from observed behavior. Interview comprehension is an input to EX-03 prototype testing, not proof that onboarding or accessibility works.

### Organizer and planner guide

1. Describe the last conference's networking workflow, attendee complaints and evidence used to judge value. What did existing tools cost in effort or money?
2. Who owns admission, privacy, moderation and support? Demonstrate how delegates get and lose access today without exposing attendee data.
3. Compare shared access with approval admission. How are leaked codes, private corporate attendance, capacity, denied applicants and cancelled events handled? Reiterate that access is not ticket entitlement.
4. Identify expected enrollment, eligible networking pool, peak simultaneous use, join spikes, concurrent events, venue connectivity and source/uncertainty for each estimate.
5. Discuss countries served, audience ages, controller/processor responsibilities, existing notices, deletion expectations and any employer/sponsor access expectations. Escalate legal determinations to privacy review.
6. Which aggregate outcomes would change renewal decisions? What denominator and response coverage would make those outcomes credible? Do not offer individual contact or assessment exports.
7. Ask about procurement authority, current spend, budget range and willingness to run a pilot. Distinguish interest, willingness-to-pay statements and an actual commitment; no billing implementation follows from this research.
8. Confirm event category, recruitment access, candidate dates, device mix, operational staffing, escalation contact and conditions for participating. Request permission before recording identities in approved storage.

### Observation worksheet

For an authorized current workflow, record participant code, context/date, task, starting condition, observed steps, elapsed time if actually measured, friction/help needed, accessibility/network constraint and outcome. Separate observation from participant explanation and researcher inference. Never record bystanders or attendee directories without approved permission. Absence of an observation is an evidence gap, not a successful task result.
